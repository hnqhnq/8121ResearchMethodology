\section{Document Class and Options}

This is the MPU Doctoral Thesis Template, Version {\em\InsertTemplateVersion}, released on Date {\em\InsertTemplateDate}.
To use this template, we start a {\LaTeX} document by \mana{documentclass\{mpThesis\}}.
This document class defaults with options: \optna{Libertine}, \optna{PCOM}, \optna{Proposal}, \optna{Review}, \optna{Screen}, \optna{NoHardback}, \optna{OneHalfSpacing}, and \optna{PhDAfterName}.
The available options are categorized as follows.

\paragraph{Font families}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item \optna{CM} --- The classic Computer Modern family.
\item \optna{Times} --- The Times Roman family.
\item \optna{Utopia} --- The Utopia family.
\item \optna{Garamond} --- The EBGaramond family.
\item \optna{Palatino} --- The Palatino family.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{Libertine} --- The Libertine family.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Programmes}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item[\DeBu] \optna{PCOM} --- Computer Applied Technology.
\item \optna{PAIDD} --- Artificial Intelligence driven Drug Discovery.
\item \optna{PETI} --- Educational Technology and Innovation.
\item \optna{JUC} --- Computer Applied Technology Joint Programme with University of Coimbra.
\item \optna{JUB} --- Computer Applied Technology Joint Programme with University of Bologna.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thesis types}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item[\DeBu] \optna{Proposal} --- Research proposals for candidature confirmation.
\item \optna{Thesis} --- Theses for thesis defences.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thesis display formats}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item[\DeBu] \optna{Blind} --- Hiding the \optna{BlindRegion} environment and \mana{BlindText}, and disabling the emphasis of author names, to support the double-blind peer review.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{Review} --- Review format with colored links and vertical rulers.
\item \optna{Final} --- Final plain format with QR code and defence panel information, no color links and no line numbers.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{Screen} --- Equal inner and outer margins, for displaying on screen.
\item \optna{Print} --- Larger inner margins. for printing and binding.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{NoHardback} --- No spine text and no calligraphic Chinese MPU watermark.
\item \optna{Hardback} --- Formal binding format, with spine text and calligraphic Chinese MPU watermark.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{OneHalfSpacing} --- $1.5\times$ line spacing in text.
\item \optna{DoubleSpacing} --- $2\times$ line spacing in text.
\item[\DeBu] \optna{PhDAfterName} --- ``Ph. D.'' below the student name on the hardcover.
\item \optna{NoPhDAfterName} --- No ``Ph. D.'' below the student name on the hardcover.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Algorithm packages}\label{par:algo-options}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item \optna{AlgoMic} --- Load the traditional \verb|{algorithm}| and \verb|{algorithmic}| packages.
\item \optna{AlgoX} --- Load the improved \verb|{algorithm}|, \verb|{algorithmicx}| and \verb|{algpseudocode}| packages.
\item \optna{AlgoIIe} --- Load the improved and combined \verb|{algorithm2e}| package.
\item \optna{MathPar} --- Load the \verb|{mathpartir}| package for type setting inference rules.
\end{itemize}
The \optna{AlgoMic}, \optna{AlgoX} and \optna{AlgoIIe} options are mutually exclusive.

\section{Emphasis of Author Names in Bibliography}

The customized \verb|{mpUnsrt.bst}| bib-style provides code to scan the author list of a bib-entry to find and highlight the specified author names.
The \verb|.bst| style file is programmed by a postfix language on a stack machine, which is quite interesting to look at.

To specify the author names to emphasize, we need to add entries in the \verb|.bib| file with category \verb|@emphasized|.
Only two fields are allowed in an entry of this category: \verb|author| and \verb|note|.
The \verb|author| field specifies the author list as in a normal category, while the \verb|note| field specifies the highlighting style,
which must be a command taking one argument of the author name, \verb|\textbf| by default. For example:
\begin{verbatim}
@emphasized{emphasized/author/list/3,
  author = {Wei Ke and David Hilbert and Gaspard Monge},
  note   = {\textbf}
}
\end{verbatim}

There can be many entries of this category, and one entry must be selected to take effect.
To select the entry of the author list, we write \verb|\EmphasizeAuthors{<entry-key>}|. For the above example:
\begin{verbatim}
\EmphasizeAuthors{emphasized/author/list/3}
\end{verbatim}
This must be done before any bib-entries and the inclusion of the \verb|.bst| and \verb|.bib| files.

By the way, we can use command \verb|\bibentry{<entry-key}| to display a bib-entry anywhere in the thesis outside the bibliography list.
This is particularly useful for listing the publication produced for the thesis defence, usually in the introduction. See \autoref{sec:publication} for example.

\section{Double-blind Review Format}

To support the double-blind peer review, option \optna{Blind} hides the student and supervisor names.
The option also disables the double degree modes.
There are still other sensitive texts that can be used to identify the student and supervisor.
They must also be hidden.
Such text can be enclosed within environment \optna{BlindRegion}:
\begin{verbatim}
\chapter{Acknowledgements}\label{ch:ack}

\begin{BlindRegion}
... paragraphs ...
\end{BlindRegion}

\subsection{Current Publication}\label{sec:publication}

\begin{BlindRegion}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
  \item \bibentry{ke2009graph}
\end{enumerate}
\end{BlindRegion}

\chapter{Biography}\label{app:biography}

\begin{BlindRegion}
... paragraphs ...
\end{BlindRegion}
\end{verbatim}
Or, such short text can be put in the \mana{BlindText} macro:
\begin{verbatim}
#    (c) 2021 \BlindText{Wei Ke and Ka-Hou Chan}
# Id: pp.py 4838 2021-11-14 12:02:46Z \BlindText{wke@IPM.EDU.MO}
\end{verbatim}
Then, if option \optna{Blind} is selected, the enclosed text is hidden, otherwise shown as normal.
At least
\begin{enumerate}
\item the {\em Acknowledgements} chapter,
\item the publication list, and
\item the {\em Biography} appendix
\end{enumerate}
must be enclosed in the blind regions.
There are also other places that the author information can occur,
for example, in the copyright declaration of your implementation code.

While the \optna{Blind} option disables the emphasis of author names in the bibliography, including the \mana{bibentry} items,
the {\textsc{Bib}\negthinspace\TeX} may not be able to recompile automatically,
you need to remove all the auxiliary files to let the recompilation happen.
